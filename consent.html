Telehealth Informed Consent

Reference copy — you will review and sign this consent in the patient portal during intake. Effective date: October 23, 2026

1. What telehealth is

Telehealth means receiving medical evaluation and treatment from a physician through secure video, audio, and electronic messaging rather than in person. Your care through GLP Tennessee is provided by William B. Merrick, DO, through William B. Merrick, DO, PLLC, to patients located in the State of Tennessee.

2. Expected benefits

Access to physician care without travel, easier scheduling, consistent monthly follow-up, and continuity with a single physician who knows your history.

3. Possible risks and limitations

  • A telehealth visit does not include a hands-on physical examination, which in some situations can limit what the physician can detect or safely treat remotely.
  • Technology can fail — video or audio may be interrupted, delayed, or of poor quality. If a connection fails, the visit will be completed or rescheduled by phone or another secure method.
  • As with any electronic system, security protocols can in rare cases be breached despite safeguards.
  • The physician may determine during any visit that your condition requires in-person evaluation, laboratory testing, or referral, and will direct you accordingly. Following that direction is part of safe treatment.

4. Alternatives

You may seek the same evaluation and treatment in person from a physician or clinic of your choice. Choosing telehealth is voluntary.

5. Prescriptions

All prescribing decisions are made solely in the physician's independent clinical judgment after evaluation. Membership does not entitle you to any particular medication. Prescriptions, when issued, are transmitted to the licensed pharmacy you designate, and medication is purchased by you directly from that pharmacy.

6. GLP-1 medications: what you are agreeing to

The medications this practice prescribes for weight management are FDA-approved semaglutide (Wegovy®) and tirzepatide (Zepbound®), dispensed by licensed pharmacies or the manufacturers' direct-to-patient programs. Where appropriate, the physician may prescribe the same medicines under their diabetes brand names (Ozempic®, Mounjaro®); using those for weight management is an "off-label" use that the physician will discuss with you. The Practice does not prescribe compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide.

These medications carry real risks. This summary does not replace the Medication Guide that comes with your prescription or the discussion you will have with the physician before anything is prescribed.

  • Thyroid C-cell tumors (boxed warning). These medicines caused thyroid tumors in rodent studies; whether they do so in people is unknown. They must not be used if you or a family member has had medullary thyroid carcinoma or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 2. Report a lump or swelling in the neck, hoarseness, trouble swallowing, or shortness of breath.
  • Pancreatitis. Stop the medication and seek care for severe, persistent abdominal pain, with or without vomiting.
  • Gallbladder disease, including gallstones, and kidney injury from dehydration when nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea are severe — keep fluids up and tell us if you cannot.
  • Low blood sugar, mainly if you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea; those doses may need to change.
  • Stomach and bowel effects — nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, reflux — are the most common side effects and usually peak around dose increases. The medicine slows stomach emptying, which can change how other oral medications are absorbed and matters before surgery or sedation: tell every surgeon, anesthesia team, and endoscopist that you take a GLP-1 medication, because you may be asked to hold a dose beforehand.
  • Eye disease. People with type 2 diabetes and existing diabetic retinopathy can see it worsen as blood sugar improves; report any vision change.
  • Mood. Report new or worsening depression, or any thoughts of harming yourself; the medication will be stopped if needed.
  • Allergic reactions, rarely severe (swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing) — call 911.
  • Pregnancy and contraception. These medicines are not for use during pregnancy, while breastfeeding, or while trying to conceive. Semaglutide should be stopped at least two months before a planned pregnancy. Tirzepatide can make birth-control pills less effective; use a non-oral or backup method for four weeks after starting and after each dose increase.
  • Weight regain. Weight commonly returns if the medication is stopped; results depend on the nutrition and activity changes you make alongside it.

You agree to take the medication only as prescribed, to attend follow-up visits so dose changes can be monitored, and to tell the physician about all other medications, supplements, and health changes.

7. Not for emergencies

Telehealth through this practice is not appropriate for emergencies. If you experience a medical emergency — including but not limited to chest pain, difficulty breathing, severe abdominal pain, or symptoms of a severe allergic reaction — call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department immediately.

8. Confidentiality and records

Your health information is protected under HIPAA and Tennessee law and is maintained in the Practice's electronic health record. You will receive the Practice's Notice of Privacy Practices in the patient portal, and you have the right to access copies of your records.

9. Your rights and responsibilities

  • You may withhold or withdraw this consent at any time without affecting your right to future care or treatment.
  • You agree to provide accurate, complete, and current information about your health, medications, allergies, and location, and to be physically located in Tennessee during visits.
  • You agree to participate in scheduled follow-up visits, which are a condition of ongoing prescribing — dose changes and refills require monitoring to be done safely.
  • You are responsible for maintaining a relationship with a local primary care provider or, if you do not have one, telling us so we can help you plan for care this practice does not provide.

10. Fees

The membership fee covers professional services as described in the Terms of Use. The Practice does not bill insurance for visits. Medication, pharmacy, and laboratory charges are separate and are paid by you directly to those providers.
